Key words

The history ideas from Unit 0 and the key words from Unit 1. Flip each card and try to say the meaning before you turn it over. Then take the quiz.

1

History ideas check

Twelve multiple-choice questions on the history ideas, drawn at random. Every round is different. Read every option before you choose: the wrong answers are close on purpose.

Workbook: page 2, Part B (which idea is it?).

2

Unit 1 facts

You need facts to explain anything. Do a round of quick-fire questions, then sort the consequences: did each one happen to Africans, or was it a gain for Europe?

Workbook: pages 3 and 4. Fill in the causes table and the four consequence boxes.

3

The 4-mark describe question

A describe question gives one mark for each accurate, relevant fact, up to four. Tick every sentence that would earn a mark. Opinions earn nothing, and facts about a different topic earn nothing.

Workbook: page 5. Fix the Leopold answer, then write two describe answers of your own.

4

Name it or explain it?

Naming a reason is Level 2. Explaining it, by saying what it led to, is Level 3. Sort each sentence, then pick the sentence that upgrades a Level 2 answer.

Workbook: page 6, the upgrade drill.

5

Build a PEEL answer (6 marks)

Tap a sentence to place it in the next empty slot. Tap a filled slot to send it back. The labels show the job each sentence does: Point, Evidence, Explain, Link. One sentence does not belong, so leave it out. Then read the model and write the twin question in your workbook.

Workbook: pages 7 and 8, Your turn T1 and T2. Remember: one reason, however good, stops at 5 marks.

6

Source skill: the message

The message is the main idea the cartoonist wants you to understand. Use See – Think – Know: what you can see, what it suggests, and what you know about the time it was made.

Workbook: pages 9 and 10, Your turn M, on a different cartoon (Source 2).

SeePick out two or three details: people, objects, labels, size.
ThinkWhat does each detail suggest? Why did the artist draw it that way?
KnowWhat was happening at the time? Who made it, when, and why?
7

Source skill: reliability

Can you trust this source? Look at the provenance (who, when, why) and check the content against what you know. A top answer gives reasons to trust it and reasons to doubt it, then makes a judgement.

Workbook: pages 11 and 12, Your turn R, on a different source (Source 4).

Who and why?Did the writer see it? Did they want to persuade, inform or entertain?
When?Written at the time, or years later? Who was in power?
Does it match?Does your own knowledge agree with what it says?
8

Source skill: compare and contrast

Find what is the same (compare) and what is different (contrast) in what the sources say. Then explain why they differ: who made each one, when, and what they wanted.

Workbook: pages 13 and 14, Your turn C, comparing Sources 3 and 4.
